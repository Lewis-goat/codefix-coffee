Samsung range and wall oven LE: low voltage

LE means: Low voltage fault (third-party label; no source gives a threshold for LE).

Applies to: range and wall oven.

Short answerThe board is not getting its low-voltage supply. A loose connector or a failing transformer on the board.
Part at faultcontrol board transformer / connectors
Severitymedium
DIY difficultyhard
When it appearsany

How to fix LE

  1. Cut power at the breaker for about 30 seconds.
  2. Check the outlet and breaker for a weak leg.
  3. If it persists, call Samsung or a qualified technician. They would check the board connectors and the low-voltage supply. Do not open the range yourself.

When to stop and call a repairer

Mains voltage on the board; cut power first.

How to check the error log

Samsung ovens do not have a user-accessible error log. The code stays on the display until you fix the cause or cut power at the breaker for about 30 seconds to 1 minute (per Samsung); if it returns after a reset, treat it as real.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.

A Samsung LE code: what is known, and what is only reported

No Samsung definition exists in what we could open

We checked Samsung's US and Canadian range error-code pages and the UK information-code page. None of them defines LE. The US page says it lists only errors with possible fixes, and both it and the Canadian page point unlisted codes to a generic procedure.

The only place we found LE named is a third-party code list, which gives it as a low voltage error. That same site has a separate entry titled Low Voltage Error that describes a DC 12 V supply dropping below 9 V, but no source we opened ties that entry to the LE code, so the threshold cannot be assumed to apply.

What the generic Samsung procedure says

For any unlisted code, Samsung's instruction is to turn the range off and on with the Power button. If the code returns, switch off the circuit breaker, wait 30 seconds, and switch it back on. If it still returns, request service through Samsung. That is the whole official path.

One listed Samsung code concerns supply: Samsung's US page lists bAd LinE as a loose power connection. LE is not documented as being the same thing, so do not assume it is. It only shows that Samsung has a message for a poor supply connection.

Reasoning about a low voltage message, labelled as inference

A message called low voltage on a range could point at the mains supply, at the connection where the range meets the supply, or at the low-voltage power inside the control. That is our reasoning from the name, not a sourced diagnosis. What can be observed without tools is whether the display flickers, whether other things on the same breaker misbehave, and whether the code appears at a particular moment such as when a heavy element switches on.

Ranges draw high current. A loose or worn supply connection is a heat and fire risk, so inspecting it is work for an electrician or Samsung service, not for an owner. The same applies to anything inside the range.

What to do

Reset once as Samsung describes. If LE does not return and the range works normally, note the date and conditions. If it returns, stop using the range for anything long or high power, and request service, telling the technician exactly what the display shows and when. Mention if the range was recently moved, reinstalled or had its supply worked on, since a supply connection is the one area where Samsung has a related message.

What a service visit would likely involve

We found no Samsung procedure for LE, so we cannot say what a Samsung technician measures. A general visit for a supply-type message would reasonably start with the supply and connection, then the control, but that is an expectation, not a documented sequence. Ask for the finding in plain terms: was the supply at the range correct, and was the connection sound. If the answer is that the control needs replacing, ask what test led to that and whether the code was reproduced.

Quick decision guide

  • If LE shows once and clears after a Power-button off and on, note it and keep watching.
  • If it returns, switch off at the breaker for 30 seconds and restore, as Samsung describes.
  • If the display flickers or other loads on the same circuit misbehave, stop and have an electrician check the supply.
  • If the range was recently moved or reinstalled, tell the technician, and have the power connection checked by a professional.
  • If LE keeps returning, request Samsung service and do not open the range.

What we could not confirm

  • We found no Samsung page that defines LE, so its meaning rests on one third-party list.
  • MyPros describes a low voltage error with a 9 V threshold in a separate entry, and no source ties it to LE or says which board or supply it refers to.

Rarely. A reset or power cycle is worth one try, but LE with a "hard" difficulty rating almost always means a failed component that has to be replaced.
