Samsung range and wall oven C-21: oven overheat shutdown

C-21 means: Oven internal temperature too high — the safety monitor tripped, most often the temperature sensor or its circuit.

Short answerThe control board saw an oven temperature above the safe window and shut heating down. Owners have reported ranges becoming dangerously hot before the code appears, so treat C-21 as a stop-cooking signal, not a nuisance. The usual cause is the temperature sensor reading wrong (or its harness); the main PCB is the second suspect.
Part at faultoven temperature sensor (thermistor)

How to fix C-21

  1. Cut power at the breaker for 5 to 10 minutes and retest once. If C-21 returns on the next heat-up, the fault is real.
  2. Unplug the range. The sensor is the probe on the back wall of the cavity; remove its two screws and pull the harness forward to disconnect.
  3. Measure the sensor with a multimeter: around 1,080 ohms at room temperature is healthy. Open circuit or a wild reading means replace the sensor.
  4. Inspect the harness connector for heat damage where it passes near the element — a melted connector gives the same code.
  5. Sensor good and it still trips: the main control board is misregulating the elements, which is a service-level repair.

When to stop and call a repairer

If the oven was overheating before the code appeared, stop using it until the sensor is verified. Capacitors on the PCB hold mains voltage.

What it costs

PartsTemperature sensor $15 to $40; harness $10 to $20; main PCB $150 to $300.
ServiceA home visit from an appliance technician runs $120 to $250 for diagnosis plus the part. Boards are the expensive part; sensors and switches are cheap.
Worth repairing?Yes — the sensor is a ten-minute screwdriver part and the most common fix.

How to check the error log

Samsung ovens do not have a user-accessible error log. The code stays on the display until you fix the cause or cut power at the breaker for 3 minutes; if it returns after a reset, treat it as real.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.
