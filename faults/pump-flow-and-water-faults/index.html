What these parts do

A pump moves water, a flow meter counts it, and level probes, floats and switches tell the board what is in the tank or boiler. Dishwashers and dryers add inlet valves, drain pumps and hoses.

How they fail

The board expected water and did not see it: the flow count is wrong, a level probe reads empty, a tank is not detected, or a drain does not clear. Air in the circuit, scale, a blockage or a leak are the usual causes.

Safe checks first

  1. Check the water tank or supply is full, seated properly and its valve opens.
  2. Look for kinks, blockages or a clogged filter in the water path, and for any sign of a leak.
  3. Descale if due. Scale on a float, probe or flow meter gives false readings.
  4. Prime or purge the circuit if the machine has run dry, then retry once.

Appliances carry mains voltage. Unplug before opening anything, and stop where a code's own page tells you to.

Questions

How many codes involve pumps, flow and water?

We cover 41 codes on pumps, flow and water across 6 brands (Breville / Sage, Jura, Philips / Saeco, De'Longhi, GE, Miele).

Can I fix these myself?

By our ratings, 13 are easy, 19 moderate and 9 hard. The cheap checks above clear a share of the easy and moderate ones; the hard ones usually need a part replaced.

Are any of these dangerous?

11 of the 41 are rated high severity, mostly where a heater or mains-powered part is involved. Stop using the machine and follow the stop advice on the code's page.
