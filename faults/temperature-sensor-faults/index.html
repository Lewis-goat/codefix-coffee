What these parts do

Most machines read temperature with an NTC thermistor, a small resistor whose resistance falls as it heats. The control board measures that resistance many times a second and shuts the heater off if the reading is impossible.

How they fail

The board rarely says "sensor is wrong". It reports a reading outside the possible range: an open circuit (a broken sensor or wire, which reads as extremely cold) or a short circuit (which reads as extremely hot). Both stop the machine heating and show a code.

Safe checks first

  1. Switch off and unplug, then let the machine cool and reach room temperature. A cold machine straight from a car or garage can trip some sensor codes.
  2. Restart once. A code that clears and stays away was a transient.
  3. If it returns at once on a warm machine, the fault is the sensor, its connector or its wire, not the heater.
  4. Opening the machine means mains voltage and often security screws. If that is not you, this is a bench repair.

Appliances carry mains voltage. Unplug before opening anything, and stop where a code's own page tells you to.

Questions

How many codes involve temperature sensors?

We cover 55 codes on temperature sensors across 5 brands (Breville / Sage, Jura, Philips / Saeco, GE, Samsung).

Can I fix these myself?

By our ratings, 4 are easy, 46 moderate and 5 hard. The cheap checks above clear a share of the easy and moderate ones; the hard ones usually need a part replaced.

Are any of these dangerous?

4 of the 55 are rated high severity, mostly where a heater or mains-powered part is involved. Stop using the machine and follow the stop advice on the code's page.
