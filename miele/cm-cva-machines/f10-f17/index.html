Miele CM / CVA F10 / F17: water not drawn in

F10 / F17 means: No water, or very little water, is being drawn in — the Miele manual wording for a water-supply fault.

Short answer. F10 and F17 share one manual entry: the machine tried to fill and failed or trickled. On countertop CM machines the cause is almost always the removable container — empty, mis-seated, or its valve sticking — and on plumbed CVA builds it moves upstream to the supply valve and filter. This is the friendliest fault in the Miele table: the manual fix is genuinely the whole fix.

How to fix F10 / F17

  1. Remove the water container, fill it with fresh tap water (cold, not distilled) and reinsert it until it locks — the manual fix, verbatim.
  2. Check the container valve seat for a stuck or dirty seal and rinse it under running water.
  3. Plumbed-in machines: confirm the shut-off valve is open and the inline filter is not clogged.
  4. Descaling the water intake is the next step if the fault recurs — scale in the valve makes it intermittent.
  5. A machine that still reports F10/F17 with a verified supply needs the intake valve or pump looked at by Miele service.

When to stop and call a repairer

Nothing dangerous — just do not run the machine dry repeatedly.

What it costs

Parts: Usually $0; intake valve $30 to $60 if it has failed.

Service: Out-of-warranty manufacturer service for a super-automatic is typically $250 to $500 including return shipping; independent espresso repairers are usually cheaper for a single part.

Worth repairing? Yes — start with the container; it is the answer nearly every time.
