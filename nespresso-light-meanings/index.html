What the lights on your Nespresso machine mean

A short guide to the blinking button lights on Nespresso's two-button Original machines, using what Nespresso's support pages say, plus our own safety notes, with a separate note for Vertuo.

Models covered: Essenza Mini, Inissia, Pixie and CitiZ. The Original Lattissima models (Lattissima Touch, Gran Lattissima), CitiZ&Milk and Creatista are not covered: we found no official table of light patterns for them, so we do not guess.

Original two-button machines

The fast blink looks the same whether the cause is descaling mode or overheating, and Nespresso's pages do not say how to tell the two apart. If you did not start descaling, treat it as overheating and let it cool.

Entering and leaving descaling mode

Hold times are not the same everywhere. Nespresso's US Essenza Mini descaling guide uses a hold of about 5 seconds on both cup buttons. A Kuwait Inissia page uses about 3 seconds on both buttons. A UAE Pixie page uses about 3 seconds to enter and about 1 second to leave. A Nespresso FAQ for several Original models says to hold both buttons for 5 to 6 seconds to exit. We give no single number because the sources disagree. Follow the guide for your exact model and region.

On the Essenza Mini, the guide says the blinking slows and then goes steady when the process ends. It recommends descaling about every 3 months or 300 capsules.

Factory reset and other model-specific cues

Regional pages describe a factory reset on the Inissia and Pixie that starts with the Lungo button held while the machine is off or being switched on. The Inissia page adds that three fast blinks confirm it. Those details come from single regional pages, so check your own manual before relying on them. The Pixie page also says a red backlight means the water tank is empty. We found no official out-of-water light for the Essenza Mini or Inissia.

We advise: unplug the machine before cleaning or descaling it. Descaling liquid is a chemical, so follow the manual for your model and keep it away from skin and eyes. A fault that keeps coming back needs Nespresso support or a qualified technician.

Vertuo machines (separate from the above)

Vertuo lights use colour and blink count, and those patterns are not the same as the Original ones above. Our Vertuo blinking lights page and our 1301-1305 codes page cover them in more detail.

For a "capsule not recognized" alert on the Vertuo Next, Nespresso's FAQ gives this order:

  1. Run the cleaning procedure, then run it a second time, to clear residue from the capsule recognition area.
  2. If it persists, unplug the machine for about 10 seconds.
  3. If it still persists, do a factory reset.
  4. Descale the machine every 3 months.

Nespresso's Vertuo Next alert list does not say which colour or blink count goes with each alert, because the app delivers them. So we do not give a colour-to-fault table for Vertuo.

Things people search for that Nespresso does not document

  • Codes in the 1300 family. We found no numbered 1300-family codes in the Nespresso pages we read. Our 1301-1305 page explains what we could and could not verify.
  • Vertuo code 1306 and "maintenance code". We could not find an official source for either.
  • "Optical sensor". We found no official Nespresso source that uses that term.
  • Original Lattissima models (Lattissima Touch, Gran Lattissima), CitiZ&Milk and Creatista patterns. We found no official table, so none is given here.

Questions

Why is my Nespresso blinking fast?

On the two-button Original machines we cover, Nespresso lists two causes for a fast, steady blink: the machine is in descaling mode, or it has overheated. If you did not start descaling, let it cool for at least 30 minutes. If you are in descaling mode and want out, follow the exit steps in the guide for your model, because the hold time differs by source.

What does a triple blink mean on a Nespresso machine?

For Essenza Mini, Inissia, Pixie and CitiZ, Nespresso describes fast triple blinking as an electronic error and tells you to contact Nespresso customer service. It gives no user fix. Our advice is to unplug the machine first.

Is there a Nespresso optical sensor, error 1306 or a maintenance code?

We could not find an official Nespresso source for any of the three. In the Nespresso pages we read, the Vertuo wording is "capsule not recognized". We do not explain 1306 or maintenance codes because we have no official source to base an answer on.

Does this page cover the Original Lattissima, CitiZ&Milk or Creatista?

No. We found no official light table for the Original Lattissima models (Lattissima Touch, Gran Lattissima), CitiZ&Milk or Creatista, so we do not guess at their patterns. Check the manual for your exact model or contact Nespresso.
