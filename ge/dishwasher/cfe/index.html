GE Appliances dishwasher CFE: continuous fill error

CFE means: Continuous fill error on GE CDT-series (2020 plastic-tub) dishwashers: the tub kept filling.

Applies to: dishwasher.

Short answerA parts site citing the GE service manual for the 2020 Plastic Tub Series (CDT-prefix models) defines CFE as Continuous Fill Error: the machine kept filling without reaching the expected level. Other sites call it a communication fault; we could not confirm that against a GE document. Treat it as a fill or level problem first, and a board problem only after that.

How to fix CFE

  1. Start/Reset, wait two minutes, then switch the dishwasher off at the breaker for about 30 seconds and restore it (GE's own reset guidance).
  2. With the machine off at the breaker, look for water still entering the tub. If it fills with power off, the inlet valve is leaking through.
  3. Check the float switch in the tub floor moves freely and is not stuck with debris.
  4. Only if the fill is normal and CFE persists, have the control boards and door harness checked.

When to stop and call a repairer

Turn off the water supply under the sink if the machine keeps filling, and disconnect power before opening anything.

Diagnosing GE CFE, step by step

What the code appears to mean

CFE is not in GE's public dishwasher code table. The only definition we could find comes from a repair-information site, MyPros+, which attributes it to a GE service manual for the 2020 plastic tub series. It reads the code as a continuous fill error: the pressure sensor reports a high water level several times in a row, and the drain pump runs in response.

That description matters because it tells you which way the fault points. This is not a low-water or communication problem. The control believes there is too much water, or that water keeps arriving. If you open the door and the tub is visibly flooded, take it at face value. If the tub is dry, the sensor reading itself is the suspect.

What to do first, with no tools

The MyPros+ entry shows only its first step publicly: shut off the water supply and work out why the inlet valve is not closing. Closing the under-sink valve is also the sensible move if the tub keeps filling. The remaining steps on that page are behind a paywall, so we cannot tell you what resistance, voltage or part checks the manual specifies.

GE's code page names a stuck float cover and debris in the sump as things to check for other codes (C4, a double fill after a power failure, and C5, a pump-out time that was too short). Looking for either is free and quick, but we have no source saying they cause CFE, so treat it as a cheap look, not a diagnosis.

For C3 and C7, GE's table says to switch power off at the breaker for about 30 seconds and then restore it. It gives no such step for CFE, but a brief power cycle is harmless and tells you whether the code was a one-off.

Where the likely faults sit

Two components decide whether the tub overfills: the water inlet valve, which admits water, and the pressure sensor with its wiring, which reports the level. The repair site's first step points at the valve, while the code definition rests on the sensor reading. GE's table has a separate PrS code for an invalid pressure sensor signal and recommends service for it, which shows GE treats that sensor circuit as a service item.

Telling these apart is easy to describe and hard to do safely. Water in the tub with the machine off and the supply open points at the valve. A dry tub points at the sensor side. Testing or replacing the sensor circuit or the inlet valve is technician work, so leave it to one.

When to stop

Do not run the machine repeatedly with a flooding tub, and do not bridge or bypass any switch or sensor to force a cycle. If the tub overfills, the valve is the first thing to isolate, and testing or replacing it, or the sensor circuit, is technician work.

Quick decision guide

  • If water is standing in the tub while the machine is idle, close the under-sink supply valve and call a technician.
  • If the tub is dry or normal and CFE shows, cut power at the breaker for about 30 seconds and run a short cycle to see whether it was a one-off.
  • If you want a free extra look, check for a stuck float cover and sump debris, which GE lists for the related C4 and C5 codes.
  • If the display shows PrS rather than CFE, GE's table recommends service for the pressure sensor signal.
  • If CFE returns after a power reset, stop using the dishwasher and book a repair; the public sources offer no further owner steps.

What we could not confirm
