Breville / Sage Barista Touch Impress ER05: temperature sensor open circuit (not detected)

ER05 means: Milk jug temperature sensor open circuit detected at startup.

The Breville / Sage Barista Touch Impress shows this code with identical meaning and fix as the Barista Touch. Full guide for ER05.

Short answerThe control board cannot read the milk jug temperature sensor (NTC). On this code the sensor reads as an open circuit: the wire has come off, the plug is loose or corroded, or the sensor itself has failed. Because it fails the self-test at power-on, the machine refuses to start until the sensor reads again. ER05 on the Barista Touch Impress is the milk jug temperature sensor failing its startup self-test, exactly as on the Barista Touch BES880. The sensor sits on the drip tray and its wire fatigues underneath.

How to fix ER05

  1. Switch the machine off at the wall, wait 60 seconds and switch it back on. A one-off reading glitch clears; a real open circuit comes straight back.
  2. Look at the milk temperature sensor on the drip tray area. The sensor knob must sit vertically in its rubber mount and the metal contact pads must line up with the jug. Reseat it, clean the pads and try again. This alone fixes a large share of ER05 to ER08 reports.
  3. Check for water where it should not be. Moisture on the board or in a connector reads as an open or short. Dry everything and look for the leak that caused it (usually a boiler o-ring or hose clip).
  4. If the code returns, replace the milk jug temperature sensor (NTC). On the Barista Touch Impress it is a plug-in part; note which way the wires route before pulling the old one.
  5. Clear the stored error afterwards (see how to read the error log below) so you can tell whether it recurs.

Reseat the sensor knob first; replace the sensor / wire kit if it returns. The BES880 milk sensor kit fits the Impress.

When to stop and call a repairer

Stop and book service if you find a swollen or burnt component on the board, or if the sensor plugs are fine and a new sensor does not clear the code: that points at the control board.

What it costs

PartsGenuine NTC sensor assemblies run about $25 to $95 depending on which sensor it is (steam wand and milk-jug sensors are the dear ones). O-ring kits are $10 to $20.
ServiceOut-of-warranty Breville service quotes for internal faults are commonly $300 to $500, sometimes more, plus shipping. Independent espresso repair shops are usually cheaper for a sensor swap.
Worth repairing?Usually yes. A sensor plus o-rings is under $100 and 30 to 60 minutes. Only a dead control board makes the repair questionable on an older machine.

How to check the error log

Unplug the machine. Press and hold the Power button while plugging it back in. When the password screen appears, enter 02015 to open Service Mode, then open the error log.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.

ER05 during startup or during use?

Breville / Sage Barista Touch Impresss report ER05 during the power-on self-test. That timing is a useful clue when narrowing the cause.

Can I keep using the machine with ER05 showing?

Usually you can finish the drink you are making, but do not ignore it — the underlying fault rarely heals itself. Work through the cheap checks first.

Will ER05 clear on its own?

Sometimes. Try the reset and cleaning steps first — they cost nothing and clear the milder cases. A code that returns within a day or two needs the parts fix.

How long does the repair take?

Thirty minutes to an hour if you are comfortable opening the case; budget an afternoon the first time.
