Breville / Sage Oracle Touch ER15: temperature sensor short circuit

ER15 means: Steam wand temperature sensor short circuit at startup.

Short answer. The steam wand temperature sensor (NTC) is reading as a short circuit: the board sees near-zero resistance, which a healthy sensor never produces. The two real-world causes are water bridging the sensor contacts (a leak or steam at the probe seal) and a failed sensor. Because it failed at startup, it is usually a wet connector or a sensor that has already died. ER15 is the steam wand temperature sensor reading as a short circuit at startup. The Oracle Touch has an NTC inside the wand that stops the auto-texturing at the right milk temperature, and it lives in the wettest place on the machine. Milk residue creeping up inside the wand or scale at the wand base bridges the sensor.

How to fix ER15

  1. Switch off at the wall and unplug. Leave the machine for an hour so any moisture in the connectors dries, then try again. A short that clears after drying tells you there is a leak to find.
  2. Inspect the steam wand where it enters the machine: scale or milk residue creeping into the wand can bridge the sensor. Deep-clean the wand and the tip; if it recurs, replace the wand assembly.
  3. Replace any leaking o-rings at the boiler fittings and NTC probes. Kits are cheap and the leak, not the sensor, is often the true fault.
  4. If the code persists after everything is dry, replace the steam wand temperature sensor (NTC).
  5. Clear the error log and run the machine through a full heat-up and a shot to confirm.

Deep-clean the wand and tip, dry the machine overnight, and retry. If ER15 comes straight back cold, the wand assembly (which includes the sensor) is replaced as one part, about $60 to $95, with a screwdriver.

ER16 is the same sensor failing during operation; ER13 and ER14 are the same sensor reading open circuit rather than shorted. Same part.

When to stop and call a repairer

If you see scorch marks on the triac board or the machine trips your GFCI / RCD, stop: that is an electrical fault, not just a sensor.

What it costs

Parts: Genuine NTC sensor assemblies run about $25 to $95 depending on which sensor it is (steam wand and milk-jug sensors are the dear ones). O-ring kits are $10 to $20.

Service: Out-of-warranty Breville service quotes for internal faults are commonly $300 to $500, sometimes more, plus shipping. Independent espresso repair shops are usually cheaper for a sensor swap.

Worth repairing? Yes in most cases. Sensor plus o-ring kit is well under $100. If a leak has soaked the control board, get a quote before spending anything.

How to check the error log

Switch the machine off at the wall. Hold the front Power button, switch the wall power back on and release when the logo appears. Enter the service password 00000, then open Error Counter (stored faults) or Live Debug (live temperatures and water levels).

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.
