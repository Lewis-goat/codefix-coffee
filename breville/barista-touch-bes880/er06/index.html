Breville / Sage Barista Touch ER06: temperature sensor open circuit (not detected)

ER06 means: Milk jug temperature sensor open circuit detected during operation.

Same code, same fix on the Barista Touch Impress. Everything below applies to both.

Applies to: Barista Touch.

Short answerThe control board cannot read the milk jug temperature sensor (NTC). On this code the sensor reads as an open circuit: the wire has come off, the plug is loose or corroded, or the sensor itself has failed. Because it dropped out mid-cycle, a loose or heat-damaged connection is the most likely cause.
Part at faultmilk jug temperature sensor (NTC)
Severitymedium
DIY difficultymoderate
When it appearswhile brewing or steaming

How to fix ER06

  1. Switch the machine off at the wall, wait 60 seconds and switch it back on. A one-off reading glitch clears; a real open circuit comes straight back.
  2. Look at the milk temperature sensor on the drip tray area. The sensor knob must sit vertically in its rubber mount and the metal contact pads must line up with the jug. Reseat it, clean the pads and try again. This alone fixes a large share of ER05 to ER08 reports.
  3. Check for water where it should not be. Moisture on the board or in a connector reads as an open or short. Dry everything and look for the leak that caused it (usually a boiler o-ring or hose clip).
  4. If the code returns, replace the milk jug temperature sensor (NTC). On the Barista Touch it is a plug-in part; note which way the wires route before pulling the old one.
  5. Clear the stored error afterwards (see how to read the error log below) so you can tell whether it recurs.

When to stop and call a repairer

Stop and book service if you find a swollen or burnt component on the board, or if the sensor plugs are fine and a new sensor does not clear the code: that points at the control board.

What it costs

PartsGenuine NTC sensor assemblies run about $25 to $95 depending on which sensor it is (steam wand and milk-jug sensors are the dear ones). O-ring kits are $10 to $20.
ServiceOut-of-warranty Breville service quotes for internal faults are commonly $300 to $500, sometimes more, plus shipping. Independent espresso repair shops are usually cheaper for a sensor swap.
Worth repairing?Usually yes. A sensor plus o-rings is under $100 and 30 to 60 minutes. Only a dead control board makes the repair questionable on an older machine.

How to check the error log

Switch the machine off at the wall. Hold the front Power button, switch the wall power back on and release when the logo appears. Enter the service password 00000, then open Error Counter to see stored faults or Live Debug for live temperatures.

Clear the log after a repair so you can tell whether the fault has really gone.

Usually you can finish the drink you are making, but do not ignore it — the underlying fault rarely heals itself. Work through the cheap checks first.
