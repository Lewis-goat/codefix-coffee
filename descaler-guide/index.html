Which descaler for which machine

Makers do not agree on what you can put in the tank. This page lists what each manufacturer's own manual or support page says: the descaler it names, any dose it gives, how often, and what it tells you to avoid. Where a maker says nothing, we say so.

We only report maker documents here. We do not recommend a brand or product, and we give no prices. Procedures and intervals differ by model and country, so treat the table as a starting point and read the manual for your exact machine.

Short notes by maker

Keurig

Keurig's pages offer its own solution, and for some brewers a vinegar method. The K-Cafe manual we read names only Keurig's solution and gives no vinegar option. Pick the page for your model, not a general tip. We could not re-open Keurig's master PDF, so we give no dose from it.

Ninja

The one Ninja manual we read, for the CM400C, allows a branded descaling solution or white vinegar. It says a cancelled cycle will not descale the machine. This was one model, and a third-party copy of the manual. Check yours.

Philips, Saeco and Gaggia

Philips names one descaler, CA6700. On vinegar, the regions differ. The US page warns against acetic acid (vinegar) in its 5000 series section. The UK page says acids are a problem for espresso machines but that filter machines without a pump may use household vinegar. Gaggia is a related brand, with its own descaling product. See our Philips and Saeco hub, the Saeco and Philips troubleshooting page and the Gaggia troubleshooting page.

Nespresso

Nespresso says to use its kit and does not recommend home remedies. Its UK page gives two slightly different intervals, so check your own model's guide. See the Nespresso hub and Nespresso light meanings.

Breville and Sage

Breville's support article says to use an approved descaler and not vinegar, but gives no dose. The dose comes from the model manual. We read the BES870 manual only. Our Breville hub lists model pages.

De'Longhi

De'Longhi gives a clear dose on its US support page and does not recommend vinegar. For light meanings see our Magnifica descale light page and the De'Longhi hub.

Jura

Jura says to use its tablets, names vinegar as a non-recommended agent, and warns that interrupting the program may damage the machine. See the Jura hub and Jura troubleshooting.

We advise: follow the safety notes in your own manual. Descalers are acids, so keep them away from skin, eyes and stone surfaces, as Jura's manual notes for marble. Gaggia's manuals say never to drink the solution or the water dispensed during the cycle. Some machines dispense hot water during descaling, so keep hands clear of the spout. A machine that will not complete a cycle needs the maker or a qualified technician.

Questions

Can I use white vinegar to descale my coffee machine?

It depends on the maker and sometimes the machine type. Keurig publishes a white vinegar method for some brewers, but its K-Cafe manual names only Keurig solution. One Ninja CM400C manual allows white vinegar. Philips US says not to use it on its 5000 series, while Philips UK says it is fine for filter machines without a pump. Jura, Gaggia (Classic Pro manual) and Breville US say not to use it, Nespresso does not recommend it, and De’Longhi does not recommend it for espresso machines. Check the manual for your exact model first.

Can I use citric acid instead of a branded descaler?

We found no maker page that gives a general citric acid method. Nespresso does not recommend it, and De’Longhi says results depend on getting the dilution right. A Keurig PDF may list a citric acid method for certain brewers, but we could not open it again to check, so we do not give amounts. Breville, Ninja, Jura, Philips and Sage do not mention citric acid in the pages we read. Where a maker is silent, we cannot say it is safe.

How often should I descale?

Makers give different answers. Keurig says every 3 to 6 months, or on some brewers when the descale light comes on. Nespresso’s UK page says every three to four months or every 300 capsules, whichever comes first. Philips UK says every 6 months and when the descaling message appears. Gaggia’s Classic Pro manual says every 2 months. De’Longhi says when the machine prompts you, and adds that it depends on water hardness and how often you brew. Breville, Jura and Ninja tie it to a light or prompt in the manuals we read.

Does using a non-branded descaler void my warranty?

We could not verify a general rule. Philips’ US page says in its 5000 series section that acid products and skipped descaling void the warranty. Sage’s blog says using vinegar instead of a descaler may void the warranty. The Gaggia Magenta Prestige and Cadorna Prestige manuals say failing to descale voids the warranty. For other makers we found no warranty wording. Read your own warranty card or ask the maker.

What does "not stated" mean in the table?

We read the page or manual and the maker did not address the point. That is not the same as approval.
